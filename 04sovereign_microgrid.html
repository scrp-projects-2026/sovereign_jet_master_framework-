1. Purpose & Energy Sovereignty Philosophy

This section defines the Microgrid Architecture for the Sovereign Bio-Solar-Thermal Campus. The Microgrid acts as the central energy management and power distribution platform, integrating all generation, electrochemical storage, thermal recovery, and operational load assets into a single coordinated off-grid power grid.

ENERGY SOVEREIGNTY PRINCIPLE

The campus operates strictly as a sovereign energy island. All electricity is generated, stored, distributed, and monitored within the internal microgrid ecosystem. Diesel generators form zero part of the design basis under any operational condition.

Core Strategic Functions
  • Energy Coordination: Dynamic real-time balancing of multi-source power generation and consumption.
  • Tiered Load Management: Automated priority-based load shedding during capacity constraints.
  • Generation Prioritisation: Maximum deployment of Solar PV to preserve LFP battery life and biogas reserves.
  • Power Quality Control: Dynamic 50 Hz / 400 V 3-Phase AC voltage and frequency stabilization.
  • Black Start Energization: Automated self-healing grid recovery sequence from dead-bus states.

2. System Energy Flow & Architecture Overview

The campus microgrid channels energy across three primary generation and storage vectors to supply six dedicated feeder networks:

↓
[ LFP BESS STORAGE BANK (Zone L) ] • [ CENTRAL MICROGRID BUS ]
↓
Livestock Feeder • Water Feeder • Energy Feeder • Admin Feeder • Academy Feeder • Expansion Feeder
Generation Asset Roles
  • Solar PV (30–60 kWp Base / 100–150 kWp Max): Powers active daylight loads and provides bulk charging for the BESS bank.
  • LFP BESS (120–200 kWh Base / 400 kWh Max): Maintains grid-forming voltage reference, smooths transient motor surges, and supports evening peak loads.
  • Biogas CHP (15–25 kW Base / 40 kW Max): Provides continuous, dispatchable 24/7 baseload electricity while delivering hydronic thermal heat to nursery and livestock pods.
Dedicated Campus Feeder Networks
  • Livestock Feeder: Barn 1 (Poultry) and Barn 2 (Swine) ventilation, feed hoppers, and lighting.
  • Water Feeder: Borehole-01 submersible pump, treatment plant, and Zone H telemetry.
  • Energy Centre Feeder: Biodigester macerators, gas scrubbers, CHP pumps, and BESS HVAC.
  • Administration Feeder: Central SCADA control room, operations offices, and archives.
  • Academy Feeder: Zone C training halls, computer labs, and innovation workshops.
  • Expansion Feeder: Phase 2 hydroponics, agrivoltaics, and DC EV charging hubs.

3. Tiered Load Architecture & Priority Logic

Electrical loads are classified into four distinct operational tiers. During emergency capacity constraints or partial system maintenance, automated load shedding follows a strict priority hierarchy:

Tiered Load Classification Matrix
Load Classification Designated Campus Facilities & Equipment Disconnection Priority
Tier 1: Critical Loads Climate Controlled Nursery (Zone F), Borehole Pump, Water Storage Controls, SCADA Master Platform, Biosecurity Interlocks. Never Shed (Protected by BESS reserve floor)
Tier 2: Essential Loads Poultry Cluster (Barn 1), Swine Cluster (Barn 2), Biodigester Macerators, CHP Auxiliaries, Security Lighting. Shed Tier 3 & 4 First (Shed only under extreme multi-day depletion)
Tier 3: Operational Loads Administration Complex offices, SCADA secondary screens, Workshop equipment, General lighting. Shed Second (Automated shed at 30% BESS SOC)
Tier 4: Discretionary Loads Inclusive Entrepreneurship Academy training halls, DC EV Charging Hub, Phase 2 Expansion reserves. Shed First (Automated shed at 45% BESS SOC)
Energy Dispatch Hierarchy

Daytime: Solar PV → Campus Active Loads → BESS Bulk Charging → CHP Standby

Nighttime: BESS Discharge + Biogas CHP Baseload → Essential Loads

Surge Peak: Solar PV + BESS Discharge + CHP Sync → Inrush Absorption
